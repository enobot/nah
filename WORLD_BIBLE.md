# World bible

Status: proposed. Nothing here is approved canon except the premise already chosen in `GAME_DESIGN.md`.

Chosen premise:

Champions are brought to a place called the Nexus. They must fight in a tournament. They do not know who brought them, why they were chosen, or whether they can leave. The true purpose of the tournament is the mystery.

## What the Nexus is

Proposed.

The Nexus is not a kingdom. It is a round of stone arenas, barracks, and locked gates that do not match any one world's architecture. The sky shows pieces of other skies. A voice, called the Clerk, reads rules and never answers why.

Public rule, told to every champion: win your bouts and you earn the way home.

Hidden claim, which the player can discover and which may be false: the Nexus is a lock. It pulls people at the moment they break, bend, or redefine an oath. The fights keep the lock shut. The prize for winning the last bout is not home. It is the job of Keeper, the person who stays and holds the lock.

Nobody the champions meet has been seen going home. Losers wake in the barracks. That matches the game's lives and respawns. A story death is not a deleted character.

## What this is not

This is not the multiverse tournament from any existing cartoon, game, or book. No borrowed rulers, sacred rules, or champion names. "Nexus" is a common word. Using it does not make this place that other fiction.

## How a custom player fits

The player is the Unwritten champion. The game does not write a separate novel for every species and class.

At the start, the player may pick one motive: survive, go home, take power, take revenge, protect someone, or learn the truth. Bosses comment on that motive and on the player's species only when a line exists for it. Missing lines use a general challenger line. The combat rules do not change.

## How the story is told

Short introductions, boss lines, set dressing, unlockable notes, and the result of a bout. Long speeches are rare. A player who skips optional talks can still finish the fights. The mystery is clearer if they listen.

## Tone

Cute and strange on the surface. The jokes are real. The oaths underneath are serious. A goblin in a stolen crown can still break your guard. Honor is argued, not awarded at the door.
