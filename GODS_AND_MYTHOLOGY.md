# Gods and mythology

## CANON

Champions bring beliefs with them. The first game does not confirm that any god is real, dead, or the organizer of the Nexus.

## PROPOSED

Each realm, if approved, has its own cult. None of these cults is the religion of the whole universe.

| Belief, if that realm is approved | What people say | What the first game may show |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wickmarket keeps no high god. Luck is a scrap you share. | A prayer is a meal handed over. | Puckle talks about hunger, not heaven. |
| Capwood says the soil remembers. | Memory is a duty. | Sporell hears old voices and might be wrong. |

These sentences are color for proposed characters. They are not a pantheon.

### Nathanael

Proposed on 2026-10-28. Not canon.

Nathanael calls himself lord of the Greek gods. He is an original character who uses an old personal name. He is not a figure taken from a modern game, film, or comic.

The ancient Greek myths are old enough that no living author owns Zeus, Athena, or the mountain called Olympus. That does not let us copy a modern game's versions, costumes, dialogue, or family soap opera. If those gods appear, they need our own personalities, and a lawyer should review the use before release. No list of them is approved. Do not add them as bosses until you name which ones you want.

What the tournament would hear: a divine lord has arrived and expects the match to prove his rank.

What is not decided: whether any of those gods are real here, whether they obey him, and whether he had any hand in the summons. He is not the organizer of the Nexus unless you promote that idea on purpose. Other realms keep their own beliefs. His claim does not erase them.

He loves Tracea, and she loves him. Their paths have not connected. She does not know he is in the tournament.

## MYSTERY

- Did any god gather the champions?
- Do two realms worship one being under two names?
- Is the Clerk a priest, a spell, or a liar?

The first game may raise one of these and refuse the answer.

## FUTURE

A later game can enter one cult's holy site and show whether the belief holds. That reveal has to be approved before it becomes CANON. It must not silently erase a belief the first game treated as true.
